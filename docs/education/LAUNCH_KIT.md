## Short public description

Helios is a 3D cosmic atlas you can explore from the Sun to nearby stars and confirmed exoplanets. Its optional Education mode adds guided journeys, questions and source inspection while keeping the universe on screen.

## Developer description

Helios is an MIT-licensed React/TypeScript application built around one Three.js scene, camera and render loop. Structured lessons reuse its existing travel, comparison and interior controls. Claim-level evidence preserves confidence and source dates, while local lesson progress requires no account or backend. The Education 1.0 candidate includes tests, educator resources and a documented review of security and accessibility limits.

Three.js provides the graphics library. OpenAI Codex was used as an AI-assisted development tool. These credits do not imply endorsement, scientific review or affiliation. External datasets, NASA imagery and upstream packages retain their own rights.

## Education-focused introduction

I built Helios to make exploring space feel connected. You can move between worlds, look inside a planet and keep going toward nearby stars in one scene. Education mode gives that exploration a purpose: four guided lessons, questions with explanations, and sources that show what is measured, modeled or still unknown. Teachers can use the text alternatives and printable activities alongside the 3D view.

## Scientific-sourcing follow-up

A convincing picture isn't the same as a measured fact. Helios keeps measured, inferred, uncertain, unknown and illustrative claims separate. Sources and snapshot dates are inspectable. Exoplanet surfaces, interior boundaries and accelerated solar activity are labeled as illustrations, with their limitations visible.

## Teacher outreach draft

Hello,

I'm developing Helios, an open-source 3D astronomy explorer, and I've prepared an Education 1.0 candidate for feedback. It includes four guided lessons about the Solar System, the Sun, planetary interiors and nearby stars/exoplanets. Learners can inspect sources and confidence labels, answer questions and use text alternatives when the spatial interaction isn't suitable.

I would value feedback on whether the explanations and activities fit your students, and on the practical accessibility and device limitations you encounter. It is not curriculum-certified, and I don't want to overstate what the current testing establishes. Teacher/student guides, limitations and source notes are included in the repository.

The application does not require student accounts or collect a class roster. If you choose to review it, please use the source repository and the approved candidate instructions. Thank you for considering it.
